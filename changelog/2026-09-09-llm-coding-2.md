Terminal-Bench 2.1 — the closest thing this slot has to a direct agentic and tool-calling measure — now puts GPT-6 Astra first at 58.2%, with Claude Fable 5.1 second at 57.9% and Opus 5 third at 51.8%. Arena WebDev tells the same story from a different direction: gpt-6-astra-max leads at 1796, ahead of claude-fable-5.1-max (1764) and claude-opus-5-max (1688). That Arena lead was first seen on 6 September and still holds on 9 September, and vals.ai independently had GPT-6 Astra top of its own Terminal-Bench 2.1 table (87.27%) and top of Code Migration. Two boards, same direction, held across dates — enough to move the title.

Opus 5 keeps the independent SWE-bench Verified aggregate at 96%, and on that one number it is still first. But vals has dropped SWE-bench Verified from its coding index as saturated, and Opus 5's terminal score falls once server-side fallbacks to Opus 4.8 on refused tasks are scored as failures. A secondary win on a benchmark the boards are retiring is not enough to hold a repo-scale agentic title.

Two challengers split the boards; Astra is the one ahead on both, so it takes the slot. Caveats worth carrying: no independent SWE-bench Verified run for Astra has appeared yet, it is priced at $10/$50 per million tokens against Opus 5's $5/$25, and refusals on bio- and cyber-adjacent work have been reported for the Astra Max tier. Opus 5 remains the cheaper default if your work trips those refusals.
