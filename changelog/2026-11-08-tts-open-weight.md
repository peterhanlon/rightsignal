Fish Audio Drama 3 would take this title on today's evidence, but the case has not yet cleared our bar for a change, so it is recorded as the challenger for now. The adjudicator's reasoning:

Breeze TTS 2 has lost this title on the numbers it won it with. On the Artificial Analysis provider-voice board it now scores 1228, 13th overall and only third among open-weight models, behind Fish Audio Drama 3 (1278) and Drama 3 Preview (1240). On the controlled-voice arena it scores 1021, fourth among open weights behind Drama 3 (1085), Ming-3.0-tts (1068) and Drama 3 Preview (1063). Drama 3 is therefore ahead on both of the boards we track for this slot, not just one, and Drama 3 appears at #7 (1275) in today's fetched top ten while Breeze does not feature at all. Note the board has been re-anchored since September, so the old 1202/1120 figures in the previous card are off-scale and not directly comparable.

Licence pushes the same way. Drama 3 ships under the Fish Audio Community Licence v1.0, free for commercial use below US$500k annual revenue, whereas Breeze's weights, derivatives and self-hosted outputs remain under the BreezeBlue Research and Non-Commercial Licence — a hosted subscription does not cover anything you generate locally.

Two caveats. We have no independent latency or VRAM figures for Drama 3 in this dossier, so if time-to-first-audio or a 12GB floor is your binding constraint, test before committing; Breeze's sub-40ms/0.32 RTF claims on a warmed H100 are still the better-documented ones, if vendor-sourced. And if you need a permissive licence outright, MIT-licensed Ming-3.0-tts (1187 provider-voice, 1068 controlled-voice) is the cleaner fallback. StepFun StepAudio 2.5 TTS drops off as a challenger: it now trails at 1214 and remains API-only.
