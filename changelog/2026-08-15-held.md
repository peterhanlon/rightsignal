Every claim on the board was re-checked against the live web: 10 titles verified, 21 supporting claim(s) corrected.

- **Best text embeddings** (high): gemini-embedding-2 has been generally available since April 2026 per the Gemini API release notes, with a non-preview model page and a deprecation warning on the preview endpoint's regional quotas. The truncation criticism is also unsupported: the model exposes output dimensions from 128 to 3,072 and L2-normalises non-default sizes. 'First natively multimodal embedding model' is imprecise — it is Google's first natively multimodal model, not the industry's first.
- **Best text embeddings** (medium): Verified only to July 2026 snapshots, and stated without the board-version caveat that makes it meaningful: KaLM's 72.32 is on the official MMTEB table, while Microsoft's Harrier-OSS-v1 reports 74.3 on the MTEB v2 multilingual split. Presenting one leader without noting the two boards are not comparable is misleading. The specific checkpoint is also the -2511 release.
- **Best text embeddings** (low): Reads as though NVFP4 is available for the 8B pick; the model card's own checkpoint badges list only 1B BF16, 1B NVFP4 and 8B BF16, so NVFP4 currently ships for the 1B sibling only. The dimension claim is also better stated as sliceable 4,096-dim vectors, which is how the card describes it.
- **Best image generation** (high): GPT Image 2 is not #1 across Artificial Analysis. It leads AA's text-to-image arena (1,370 v Reve 2.1 at 1,326), but on AA's image-editing arena it is second at 1,258 behind Reve 2.1 at 1,261, with MAI-Image-2.5 at 1,257 — overlapping rank ranges, so no clear margin at all. The Arena claims (T2I 1381, image-edit 1463, both #1) do check out.
- **Best image generation** (medium): Attribution to Arena is unverified. The 'largest lead in Image Arena history' framing appears only in secondary vendor coverage; I found no Arena primary statement making that claim, and Arena's live board publishes no such historical record. The launch date of 21 April 2026 is otherwise sound.
- **Best image generation** (medium): The Elo figures are right but 'neither statistically close' is misleading: Reve 2.1 actually leads Artificial Analysis's image-editing arena, three Elo ahead of GPT Image 2 and inside both confidence intervals. The note also omits that MAI-Image-2.6 is a preview scored on only ~3,500 votes, so its 1336±11 is far less settled than the leader's 1381±5.
- **Best image generation** (medium): No caveats recorded, despite material qualifications: the editing lead is a statistical tie on Artificial Analysis, GPT Image 2 is the most expensive of the front-runners, and two of the challengers are preview/preliminary entries whose scores may move.
- **Best inference serving** (high): The 70B leadership claim is contradicted by the 2026 benchmarks it appears to lean on: Spheron's March 2026 H100 run (Llama 3.3 70B FP8, unique prompts) has SGLang ahead of vLLM at every concurrency level tested, by 2.5-4.6% (1,920 vs 1,850 tok/s at 50 concurrent; 2,460 vs 2,400 at 100 concurrent), with SGLang also 2-7% lower on p50 TTFT. Only a single Runpod-cited DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B single-turn test shows vLLM ahead, and it reverses as soon as cache hits appear. The '10-20%' band is separately imprecise: the like-for-like 70B gap is low single digits, while the 8B shared-prefix gap is ~29%.
- **Best inference serving** (low): Accurate as stated (16,200 vs 12,500 tok/s on Llama 3.1 8B per AIMultiple/PremAI), but it reads as if the 29% is general to shared-prefix work; the same sources show the gap shrinking to low single digits at 70B and reversing on single-turn cache-miss traffic. Worth tightening on the next pass rather than a correction now.
- **Best LLM for coding** (medium): None of the Artificial Analysis or Arena placements could be substantiated on the live boards, and the 'crown moved on launch day' framing is unsourced — a current tracker (Software Secrets) still ranks GPT-5.6 Sol first. The verifiable parts of the claim (vals.ai SWE-bench 97.0%, half Fable 5's price) stand, so the why should rest on those.
- **Best LLM for coding** (medium): The 89.5/89.1 pair could not be found on any live board, and the frozen-board line is unverified. On the independent run I could read, vals.ai's Terminal-Bench 2.1, the gap is larger than 'a hair' (85.77% vs 84.64%), and vals discloses that Opus 5 used Claude Opus 4.8 as a refusal fallback — scoring those as failures cuts it to 81.27%. The caveats also miss that DeepSeek V4 Pro (0813) has closed to 96.40% on SWE-bench Verified since this slot was published.
- **Best LLM for coding** (low): Repeats the unverifiable AA figures and the 'joint-first on the AA Coding Index' placement. The substantive point survives on figures that can be checked, but the numbers quoted should be replaced with the vals.ai ones.
- **Best open-weight LLM** (high): The released Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B checkpoint is text-only — its model card states multimodal inputs are not supported and thinking cannot be disabled, with vision input and the 1M default context reserved for the hosted Qwen3.8-Max. It is therefore strictly less multimodal than Kimi K3, which ships native vision. Its native context is 262,144 tokens, not 1M. The '2 points behind on AA' figure (58 vs 60) is correct.
- **Best open-weight LLM** (medium): These Arena figures could not be reproduced against the live board; the only traceable text-board number for K3 is ~1486 from mid-July coverage, before and around Arena's July re-baseline, and the GLM-5.2-max #33 placement has no primary support. The sentence is also self-contradictory as published — it calls K3 the top open-weights entry while conceding Arena does not classify its licence as open. The AA score of 60 is current and correct.
- **Best open-weight LLM** (low): Imprecise on both sides. The MaaS trigger is aggregate revenue above $20M over any consecutive 12 months — not obviously 'large' — and the caveat omits the section 4 carve-out that exempts purely internal use from both the attribution and the separate-agreement duties. The 100M MAU / $20M-per-month attribution thresholds are accurate.
- **Best overall LLM** (high): Fable 5 does not lead Terminal-Bench 2.1. Vals.ai's 13 Aug 2026 update has GPT-5.6 Sol first at 85.77% ahead of Claude Opus 5 at 84.64%, with Fable outside the top two; pricepertoken's 12 Aug snapshot likewise ranks GPT-5.6 Sol above Fable; a third tracker lists Claude Mythos 5 top at 88.0%. No source places Fable first.
- **Best overall LLM** (medium): The #1 placing is well supported, but the specific figure is not. Arena's text board carries an Aug 12, 2026 snapshot date; published figures for Fable range from 1508.6 to roughly 1525, and the 10 July snapshot had it at 1505 plus or minus 8. Nothing I could reach reports 1506, so the number should be dropped or sourced to a dated Arena export.
- **Best overall LLM** (medium): The named runner-up looks stale and misidentified. The 10 July snapshot listed claude-opus-4-6-thinking (not -high) in second, and August coverage describes the chasing cluster as Claude Opus 4.8, GPT-5.5 Pro and Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview. Opus 4.6 sits three releases behind current Anthropic models, so the specific head-to-head figures cannot be relied on.
- **Best overall LLM** (low): 'Continuously' is not supportable: Arena is reported to have re-baselined Fable's score on 12 July so that only post-restoration votes counted, after which it settled back on top. The ranking history therefore has a methodology break in it.
- **Best overall LLM** (low): Partly uncorroborated. The mid-August Artificial Analysis snapshot I could reach gives Grok 4.6 60.9 (against Fable 62.1, a gap of about 1.2), and I found no source confirming the 12 August release date, the $2/$6 pricing, or that GPT-5.6 Sol scores exactly 61 on the same index.
