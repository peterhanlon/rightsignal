The fit question that kept both challengers at arm's length has been answered, and it splits two ways.

On Artificial Analysis's small open-models board, Nanbeige4.3-33B leads at 312 with Qwen3.8 27B (Xhigh) second at 303, K2 Horizon 32B a new entrant at #4 (285), and our pick nowhere in the top ten ([standings, 22 Nov 2026](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/open-source/small)). On raw index score the pick is behind, and we are not pretending otherwise.

But this slot is judged on quality per GB, tool-calling under quantisation and a 16GB RAM fit. Nanbeige is out on the third: it has shipped no quants itself, and release-day community GGUFs publish 23.5GB at Q5_K_M and 30.1GB at Q6_K, so a dense 33B does not fit at usable precision, with no tool-calling-under-quantisation figures either.

Qwen3.8 27B is the serious challenger. Our old note that it had no 4-bit build was wrong: Unsloth refreshed its GGUFs to Dynamic V3.1 on 11 November 2026, documented tool-calling parsing fixes and published quantised results. Its own guidance, though, puts 4-bit at 16-19GB of total memory — 17GB headline — against 12-14GB at 3-bit. That is over budget at the precision that earns the score, and a dense 27B decodes far slower than the pick's 3.8B active parameters.

[Gemma 4 26B A4B](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it) stays, measured at 14.25GB in Unsloth's dynamic build with 85.63% retention against 70.20% for a naive Q4_0 of the same QAT checkpoint. The title is now genuinely contested rather than comfortable; if Qwen lands a credible sub-16GB build at retained quality, it takes the slot.
